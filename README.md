# Healthcare AI Governance Operating Model for GlobalPharm International
## Executive Problem Statement

**Organization:** GlobalPharm International  
**Project:** Global Healthcare AI Governance Operating Model  
**Document Owner:** AI Governance & Risk Management  
**Document Type:** Executive Problem Statement  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Proposed  



1. Executive Summary

GlobalPharm International is rapidly adopting artificial intelligence (AI), including machine learning, generative AI, predictive analytics, clinical decision-support technologies, and AI-enabled agents across research, drug development, clinical trials, manufacturing, pharmacovigilance, regulatory affairs, healthcare services, and corporate operations.

While these technologies create significant opportunities to improve drug discovery, operational efficiency, clinical research, patient support, safety monitoring, and decision-making, they also introduce risks that may not be adequately addressed through traditional cybersecurity, privacy, IT, compliance, and enterprise risk-management processes alone.

AI systems can introduce unique risks involving patient safety, model performance, bias, explainability, data quality, privacy, cybersecurity, intellectual property, regulatory compliance, third-party dependencies, human oversight, model changes, and autonomous decision-making.

GlobalPharm currently lacks a single, globally consistent AI governance operating model that establishes how AI systems are identified, inventoried, classified, assessed, approved, monitored, changed, and retired throughout their lifecycle.

The organization therefore requires a risk-based, globally scalable AI Governance Operating Model that integrates AI-specific governance with existing enterprise cybersecurity, privacy, quality, regulatory, clinical, and risk-management structures.

The proposed operating model will establish a consistent global baseline while allowing jurisdiction-specific regulatory requirements to be applied according to the AI system's intended use, data, geography, product classification, and risk profile.



2. Business Context

GlobalPharm operates in highly regulated healthcare and pharmaceutical environments where the safety, quality, integrity, confidentiality, and reliability of information and decisions are critical.

AI may be used across the organization's lifecycle, including:

- Drug discovery and development
- Preclinical research
- Clinical trial design and recruitment
- Clinical data analysis
- Medical writing
- Regulatory submissions
- Pharmacovigilance and safety signal detection
- Manufacturing and quality operations
- Patient and healthcare-provider support
- Medical information
- Enterprise knowledge management
- Cybersecurity
- Software development
- Business operations
- Generative AI applications
- Autonomous or semi-autonomous AI agents

The increasing use of AI creates a need for governance that can distinguish between low-risk productivity applications and higher-risk systems that may influence clinical, patient, regulatory, or safety-related decisions.



3. Problem Statement

GlobalPharm does not currently have a unified global mechanism for governing AI throughout its lifecycle.

As a result, AI systems may be developed, procured, deployed, or modified without consistent processes for:

1. Identifying AI systems across the enterprise
2. Establishing accountable business and technical ownership
3. Determining intended use and context of use
4. Classifying AI systems according to risk
5. Determining applicable laws, regulations, standards, and internal requirements
6. Assessing patient, clinical, privacy, cybersecurity, regulatory, ethical, and operational risks
7. Establishing appropriate human oversight
8. Evaluating model performance and reliability
9. Assessing third-party AI providers and foundation-model dependencies
10. Documenting AI governance decisions
11. Monitoring AI systems after deployment
12. Managing material model, data, vendor, and intended-use changes
13. Responding to AI-related incidents
14. Demonstrating governance effectiveness to executives, regulators, auditors, and other stakeholders
15. Retiring AI systems in a controlled manner

Without a consistent governance model, the organization may experience fragmented decision-making, inconsistent risk treatment, regulatory exposure, duplicated controls, uncontrolled AI adoption, and difficulty demonstrating that AI systems are being managed responsibly throughout their lifecycle.

4. Key Governance Risks

### Patient and Clinical Risk

AI outputs may influence clinical, patient-support, safety, or healthcare-related decisions. Incorrect, unreliable, biased, or poorly understood outputs could contribute to patient harm or inappropriate decisions.

### Regulatory Risk

Different AI applications may fall under different regulatory requirements depending on jurisdiction, intended use, product classification, data involved, and whether the AI supports regulated activities.

### Privacy Risk

AI systems may process protected health information, personal data, clinical data, genomic information, employee information, or other sensitive information.

### Cybersecurity Risk

AI systems introduce additional attack surfaces involving models, APIs, prompts, training data, inference environments, plugins, agents, integrations, credentials, and third-party services.

### Model Risk

AI systems may produce inaccurate, unstable, biased, non-reproducible, or poorly understood outputs.

### Data Risk

AI performance and reliability may be affected by incomplete, inaccurate, biased, outdated, poorly governed, or improperly sourced data.

### Third-Party Risk

AI vendors, cloud providers, foundation-model providers, software suppliers, and external data providers may become critical components of the organization's AI supply chain.

### Human Oversight Risk

Higher-impact AI applications may require clearly defined human review, escalation, intervention, override, or approval mechanisms.

### Change Risk

AI systems may change through model updates, retraining, new data, new vendors, new integrations, changed intended use, or increased autonomy.

### Ethical and Trust Risk

AI systems may create concerns involving fairness, transparency, accountability, explainability, accessibility, and appropriate use.



## 5. Governance Objective

The objective of the Global Healthcare AI Governance Operating Model is to establish a consistent, risk-based, lifecycle-oriented approach for governing AI across GlobalPharm.

The operating model will enable GlobalPharm to:

- Identify AI systems
- Establish accountability
- Determine regulatory applicability
- Classify AI risk
- Assess AI impacts and risks
- Design appropriate controls
- Establish human oversight
- Approve or reject AI use cases
- Monitor AI performance and risk
- Manage material changes
- Respond to AI incidents
- Maintain governance evidence
- Continuously improve AI governance
- Retire AI systems responsibly

The model will be designed to support responsible innovation rather than prevent appropriate use of AI.



## 6. Governance Principles

The operating model will be based on the following principles:

### 1. Risk-Based

Governance requirements will be proportional to the AI system's potential impact and risk.

### 2. Human-Centric

AI will support human decision-making and organizational objectives while maintaining appropriate human accountability and oversight.

### 3. Lifecycle-Based

Governance will apply from AI ideation and procurement through development, deployment, monitoring, modification, and retirement.

### 4. Context-of-Use Driven

The intended purpose and context in which an AI system is used will be central to determining its governance requirements.

### 5. Regulatory-Aware

Applicable legal and regulatory requirements will be determined based on jurisdiction, data, product, use case, and risk rather than assuming that one regulation applies universally.

### 6. Secure by Design

AI governance will integrate cybersecurity, privacy, identity, access management, data protection, resilience, and supply-chain security.

### 7. Evidence-Based

Governance decisions and control effectiveness will be supported by documented evidence.

### 8. Accountable

Every AI system will have clearly defined business, technical, risk, compliance, and governance responsibilities.

### 9. Continuously Monitored

AI governance will not end when a system is approved for deployment.

### 10. Continuously Improved

AI governance processes will evolve as AI technology, regulations, organizational risk, and industry practices change.



## 7. Framework Alignment

The operating model will use multiple frameworks and regulatory sources rather than treating one framework as sufficient.

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework

NIST AI RMF will provide the primary AI risk-management structure through:

- Govern
- Map
- Measure
- Manage

### ISO/IEC 42001

ISO/IEC 42001 will provide the AI Management System perspective, supporting organizational policies, processes, accountability, risk management, and continual improvement.

### Healthcare and Pharmaceutical Governance

Healthcare and pharmaceutical requirements will be incorporated according to the relevant AI use case, including patient safety, clinical research, drug development, pharmacovigilance, manufacturing, medical products, and regulated decision-making.

### Privacy and Security

Privacy, cybersecurity, data governance, and information-security requirements will be integrated into the AI lifecycle rather than treated as separate activities.

### Jurisdiction-Specific Regulation

The operating model will maintain jurisdiction-specific regulatory modules so that AI systems are evaluated according to the laws and regulatory requirements applicable to their:

- Geography
- Intended use
- Data
- Product
- Users
- Risk
- Regulatory classification


## 8. Expected Business Outcomes

Implementation of the operating model is expected to provide:

### Improved Risk Visibility

A centralized AI inventory will provide leadership with visibility into the organization's AI footprint.

### Consistent Risk Decisions

A standardized AI risk methodology will reduce inconsistent assessments between business units and regions.

### Improved Regulatory Readiness

Documented applicability assessments, risk assessments, approvals, controls, and monitoring activities will strengthen regulatory and audit readiness.

### Reduced AI-Related Risk

AI-specific controls will address risks that may not be adequately captured through traditional technology risk processes.

### Responsible AI Adoption

The organization will be able to adopt useful AI technologies while applying appropriate safeguards based on risk.

### Improved Executive Oversight

Leadership will receive measurable information regarding AI adoption, risk exposure, incidents, control effectiveness, and remediation.

### Scalable Global Governance

A common global baseline will allow local jurisdictions and business units to apply additional requirements where necessary.


9. Definition of Success

The AI Governance Operating Model will be considered successful when GlobalPharm can demonstrate that:

- Material AI systems are identified and inventoried.
- AI systems have accountable owners.
- AI use cases have defined intended purposes and contexts of use.
- AI risk is classified consistently.
- Applicable regulations and standards are identified.
- Appropriate risk assessments are completed.
- Required controls are implemented.
- Higher-risk systems receive enhanced review.
- Human oversight requirements are documented.
- AI performance and risk are monitored after deployment.
- Material changes trigger appropriate reassessment.
- AI incidents can be identified, escalated, investigated, and remediated.
- Governance decisions are supported by auditable evidence.
- Executive leadership can understand the organization's AI risk posture.


10. Executive Decision Requested

GlobalPharm leadership should authorize the development and implementation of a Global AI Governance Operating Model.

The model should establish:

1. Enterprise AI governance accountability
2. A global AI inventory
3. AI risk classification
4. AI impact and risk assessment
5. Regulatory applicability assessment
6. AI control requirements
7. Governance approval processes
8. AI lifecycle management
9. AI incident management
10. AI change management
11. Continuous monitoring
12. Executive reporting

The operating model should be implemented using a risk-based approach that supports responsible AI innovation while protecting patients, research participants, employees, customers, company data, intellectual property, and the organization's regulatory standing.


11. Project Outcome

The final deliverable will be a scalable Global Healthcare AI Governance Operating Model that connects:

**AI Strategy → Governance → Risk → Compliance → Privacy → Cybersecurity → Quality → Regulatory → Operations → Monitoring**

This model will provide GlobalPharm with a repeatable mechanism for determining:

> **What AI are we using?**
> **Why are we using it?** 
> **What could go wrong?** 
> **Who is accountable?**
> **Which regulations apply?**
> **What controls are required?**
> **Who approves it?**  
> **How do we know it remains safe and compliant?**

This operating model will establish the governance foundation required for responsible and scalable AI adoption across the global organization.
